Coffee Fundamentals

The foundation: what coffee is, how it is processed and roasted, and how those choices shape extraction.

1. The coffee plant

Commercial coffee mainly comes from Coffea arabica and Coffea canephora (robusta). Variety, altitude, climate, soil and farming all influence the seed before it reaches the roaster.

Arabica

  • Often more aromatic and acidic
  • Many varieties and flavor profiles
  • Generally higher market value

Robusta

  • Higher caffeine
  • More body and bitterness
  • Common in espresso blends

2. Processing

MethodWhat happensTypical cup direction
WashedFruit removed before dryingClean, bright, transparent
NaturalWhole cherry dries around the seedFruity, sweet, heavier
HoneySome mucilage remains during dryingSweet, balanced, varied

3. Roasting & grinding

Roasting drives browning reactions and creates the aromas we associate with coffee. Grind size controls how quickly water can access the coffee particles.

Rule of thumb: finer grind generally increases extraction and slows flow; coarser grind generally decreases extraction and speeds flow.

Dial-in tree

Too fast / sour / thin
  • Grind finer
  • Check distribution and puck prep
  • Confirm dose is consistent
  • Taste again before changing multiple variables
Too slow / bitter / dry
  • Grind coarser
  • Check for over-dosing or restricted basket
  • Inspect puck prep for fines migration
  • Retest with the same dose and ratio

Puck preparation

  • Weigh the dose consistently
  • Distribute grounds evenly
  • Use WDT when appropriate
  • Tamp level and repeatably
  • Lock in and brew promptly

Milk & Latte Art

Texture is the foundation. Latte art is controlled movement of well-textured milk.

Milk steaming

  • Start with cold fresh milk in a clean pitcher
  • Introduce air gently near the surface
  • Create a rolling vortex to integrate foam
  • Stop before milk becomes overheated
  • Tap and swirl before pouring
Target: silky, glossy microfoam with no visible large bubbles. A common working range is roughly 55–65 °C, depending on milk and cafe standard.

Pouring progression

01

Heart

Start high to mix, lower to build the white base, then lift and cut through.

02

Tulip

Layer small pushes of milk while controlling pitcher height and flow.

03

Rosetta

Use a steady wiggle with controlled forward movement and a final cut.

Coffee Origins

Origin is not a flavor guarantee. Variety, processing, roast and brewing all matter.

AFRICA

Ethiopia

Known for diverse heirloom populations and often floral, citrus, stone-fruit or tea-like profiles.

AFRICA

Kenya

CENTRAL AMERICA

Guatemala

Highland coffees can show cocoa, spice, citrus and stone-fruit notes.

SOUTH AMERICA

Colombia

Extremely diverse by region; common profiles include caramel, red fruit, citrus and chocolate.

ASIA

Some wet-hulled coffees show earthy, herbal, spicy and full-bodied character.

SOUTH AMERICA
